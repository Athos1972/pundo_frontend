The keys are in your hand, the fridge is empty, and the air conditioner is already working harder than you expected. This Cyprus utilities guide helps you handle the practical setup behind a comfortable home: electricity, water, internet, gas, and local waste charges. The exact process changes by municipality, landlord, and property type, but knowing what to ask for before move-in can save days of calls and a few expensive surprises.

## Start with the rental agreement and meter readings

Before you open an account or pay a transfer fee, check how utilities are handled in your lease. In Cyprus, some landlords keep electricity and water accounts in their own name and recharge the tenant. Others expect you to transfer services directly. Neither arrangement is automatically better, but it needs to be clear in writing.

Ask for a photo of the electricity and water meter readings on the day you receive the keys. If the property has separate meters, make sure the meter numbers match the numbers on the latest bills. This is especially useful in apartments, older buildings, and properties with shared outdoor areas, where confusion over a meter can become a frustrating end-of-tenancy dispute.

You should also ask these questions before signing or immediately after moving in: Are there unpaid balances on any account? Is the property connected to the sewerage network? Who pays communal fees and garbage charges? Is there a working internet line already installed? If a landlord says a bill is included, ask whether there is a monthly cap. An "all bills included" arrangement can be convenient, but it may come with a limit that matters during Cyprus's long cooling season.

## Electricity in Cyprus: the account that needs attention first

Electricity is generally supplied through the Electricity Authority of Cyprus, commonly called EAC. For most renters, setting up or transferring electricity is the first priority because it affects almost everything else, from hot water to cooking and air conditioning.

You will typically need identification, proof that you are entitled to use the property, and a completed application or transfer request. The account holder may also need to pay a deposit. Deposit amounts and procedures can vary, so confirm the current requirement directly rather than relying on a former tenant's experience.

### Do not assume the power bill will be small

Electricity costs can feel high in a home with frequent air conditioning, electric water heating, older appliances, or poor insulation. A compact newer apartment may use far less than a detached house with several units running all afternoon. Your lifestyle matters too: someone working from home through July and August will consume differently from someone who is out all day.

When you view a property, look for practical clues. Count the air-conditioning units, ask whether they have been serviced, and check whether the water heater uses solar panels, electricity, or both. Cyprus gets abundant sun, and solar water heating is common, but it does not eliminate the need for an electric immersion heater on cloudy days or when hot water demand is high.

If you are moving into a shared home, agree early on how bills will be divided. Splitting evenly is simple, but it can feel unfair if one person works from home or uses the air conditioning much more. A brief conversation is easier than trying to reconstruct usage from a bill later.

## Water service depends on where you live

Water administration in Cyprus is local. Depending on the address, your account may be handled by a municipality, a community council, or another local water authority. This means the documents, payment methods, and billing rhythm can differ between Larnaca, Limassol, Nicosia, Paphos, and nearby villages.

Your landlord or agent should be able to tell you which office manages the property. Request a recent bill and confirm whether you need to transfer the account, register as a tenant, or simply reimburse the owner. In some rentals, water remains in the landlord's name because local transfer procedures are less straightforward than electricity.

### Learn what is on the bill

A water bill may include more than water consumption. Depending on the area, it can include sewerage-related charges or other local fees. Garbage collection and municipal charges may arrive separately, be included in communal building fees, or be paid through the property owner. The key is not to assume that one bill covers everything.

Water is safe for many everyday uses, but drinking preferences vary. Some households drink tap water, while others use filtered water or buy bottled water. If you choose a filter, consider how often replacement cartridges are needed and whether the building's plumbing is older. A local hardware or home-goods shop can help you find fittings and filtration options that match your kitchen tap.

## Internet: check availability before choosing a package

Internet quality in Cyprus can be excellent in one building and limited in the next. Fiber availability, existing cabling, building access, and provider coverage all matter more than a package advertised for a neighborhood.

Ask the landlord whether the apartment has an active line and which provider was used previously. If a line is already installed, activation may be relatively quick. If a technician needs to bring service into the building, timing can depend on permissions, infrastructure, and appointment availability.

For remote workers and digital nomads, do not choose solely by headline speed. Ask about upload speed, contract length, installation charges, router terms, and what happens if you leave Cyprus earlier than planned. A mobile data plan can be a useful short-term bridge, particularly during your first week, but it may not suit video calls, cloud backups, or a household with several connected devices.

Mobile providers offer prepaid and contract options, and a local SIM can make delivery coordination, banking verification, and everyday calls easier. Bring your passport or other accepted identification when registering. If your phone supports dual SIM or eSIM, keeping your home number active while using a Cyprus number can reduce friction during the transition.

## Gas, heating, and hot water are not one-size-fits-all

Many homes in Cyprus use bottled LPG for cooking rather than a mains gas connection. Your stove may run from a cylinder stored outside or in a designated ventilated area. Never improvise connections or store cylinders indoors. Ask the landlord which supplier serves the property, where the shutoff valve is, and whether the cylinder is included in the tenancy.

Some apartments have electric ovens but gas cooktops; others are fully electric. Winter heating also varies widely. You may find split-unit air conditioners, portable heaters, fireplaces, or central systems in certain homes. Cyprus winters are milder than those in much of Europe or North America, but indoor spaces can feel cold when buildings are not insulated or heated consistently.

Check the hot-water setup on your first day. If there is a solar system, learn which switch controls the electric backup heater and turn it off after use unless the system is designed otherwise. This small habit can make a noticeable difference to your electricity use.

## Waste, recycling, and apartment communal fees

Waste collection rules are set locally, so bin colors, pickup days, and recycling arrangements can vary. Your building manager, landlord, or neighbor can usually tell you where the bins are and whether there are separate containers for paper, plastic, glass, or general waste.

If you live in an apartment complex, communal fees deserve the same attention as utility bills. They can cover cleaning, lift maintenance, shared lighting, water for common areas, management, and sometimes garbage charges. Ask for the monthly amount, what is included, and whether there are expected one-off costs. A low rent can be less of a bargain once building fees and high summer electricity use are added.

For household essentials, local discovery matters. Finding a nearby electrician, plumber, internet installer, hardware shop, or water-filter supplier is often more useful than waiting for a distant recommendation. ## Keep a simple utility folder from day one

Save copies of your lease, account numbers, meter photos, deposit receipts, and the last bill from every service. A phone folder is fine, but label files clearly with the property address and date. If you move again, these records make account closures, deposit requests, and final bill checks much simpler.

Also set payment reminders. Some providers offer online payment, while others may use bank payment, local payment points, or billing portals. Missing a due date is rarely worth the stress, especially when you are still settling into a new city.

A home in Cyprus becomes easier to enjoy once the background tasks are sorted. Take one utility at a time, ask for evidence rather than verbal assurances, and give yourself a small buffer before the first bill arrives. That leaves more time for the good part of settling in: learning your neighborhood and finding the places that make it feel like yours.
