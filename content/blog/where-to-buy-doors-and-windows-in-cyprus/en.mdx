A window that looks perfect in a showroom can feel very different after its first Cypriot summer. Strong sun, high temperatures, dust, wind, and coastal salt all put pressure on frames, glazing, seals, and hardware. That is why knowing **where to buy doors and windows in Cyprus** is only part of the decision. You also need a supplier who understands your property, location, and installation requirements.

For a rental refresh, a single replacement door, or a full renovation, Cyprus has options ranging from local fabricators to large showroom suppliers. The best place to start depends on whether you prioritize price, custom sizing, energy performance, lead time, or an installer who can manage the whole job.

## Start with local window and door showrooms

For most home projects, visit a local showroom before choosing a product. Photos and online catalogs are useful for style ideas, but they cannot show how a sliding door moves, how solid a lock feels, or whether the frame finish suits your home. In a showroom, you can compare colors, opening mechanisms, handles, insect screens, and glazing side by side.

Larnaca, Limassol, Nicosia, and Paphos all have suppliers specializing in aluminum systems, uPVC windows, entrance doors, shutters, and glass installations. Larger cities usually offer the broadest choice, while smaller towns may have independent workshops that provide more personal service and custom work. If you live outside an urban center, ask whether delivery and installation are included in the quoted area.

A local discovery platform such as Pundo can make the first search easier, especially if you are new to Cyprus and want to find nearby shops in a language you are comfortable using. Once you have a shortlist, call ahead to confirm which products are on display. Some businesses show standard window samples but make doors, pergolas, or larger glass systems only to order.

## Where to buy doors and windows in Cyprus by project type

The right supplier is often determined by the job rather than the city.

### For replacement windows in an apartment

Look for a supplier that can measure existing openings accurately and coordinate installation around occupied rooms. Apartment replacements are rarely as simple as removing an old frame and fitting a new one. Exterior finishes, shared-building rules, balcony access, and the need to keep your home secure overnight can affect the work.

Aluminum is a common choice in Cyprus because it handles heat well and comes in many slim, modern profiles. Ask about thermal-break aluminum if reducing heat transfer and improving year-round comfort are priorities. It usually costs more than standard aluminum, but can be worthwhile in homes with extensive glazing or strong afternoon sun.

uPVC can also offer good insulation and value, particularly for bedrooms and smaller windows. Its appearance and performance vary significantly by profile and installation quality, so do not compare quotes on frame material alone.

### For a new-build home or major renovation

Choose a supplier early, ideally alongside your architect, engineer, or contractor. Doors and windows influence wall openings, floor levels, drainage, insulation details, shading, and the electrical planning for shutters or smart locks. Leaving them until late in the build can create expensive compromises.

For these projects, look for firms that offer site measurement, technical drawings, fabrication, installation, and after-sales support. A supplier that works directly with your construction team can help prevent common problems, such as a sliding system sitting too high above finished flooring or a door opening in the wrong direction.

Ask for product specifications in writing, including frame series, glass type, color, hardware, and warranty. “Double glazing included” is not enough detail when you are comparing large quotes.

### For entrance doors and security upgrades

A front door is both a design choice and a security decision. Specialized door suppliers may offer aluminum, steel, composite, timber, or security-rated entrance doors with multipoint locking systems. For homes that are vacant for part of the year, dependable locks, reinforced hinges, and a professional installation may matter more than choosing the most decorative panel.

Ask whether the quoted price includes the cylinder, handle set, threshold, closer, and any required side panels. These details can change the final cost quickly. If the door is exposed to direct sun, check how the finish is expected to perform over time and whether its warranty covers that exposure.

### For coastal homes

In Larnaca, Limassol, Paphos, Protaras, and other coastal areas, salt air deserves special attention. It can corrode lower-quality fittings and affect moving parts long before the frame itself shows a problem. Choose hardware designed for coastal conditions, and ask what maintenance the supplier recommends for tracks, hinges, locks, and exterior finishes.

This does not automatically mean you need the most expensive system. A well-specified mid-range product with suitable hardware and regular cleaning can be a better fit than an impressive-looking option that is poorly installed or difficult to maintain.

## Compare quotes beyond the bottom line

Get at least two or three detailed quotes for significant work. The lowest number may exclude removal of old frames, plaster repairs, exterior sealing, insect screens, delivery, VAT, or installation. A quote that seems more expensive can be better value if it includes a complete, accountable service.

When comparing suppliers, make sure each quote covers the same scope. Check the number and type of windows, opening direction, glass specification, frame color, handles, shutters or screens, and installation work. If one supplier uses a different frame system or glass thickness, the prices are not directly comparable.

Lead times also matter. Custom aluminum or uPVC products are commonly made to order, and imported doors may take longer. Before paying a deposit, ask for an estimated production and installation date, what could delay it, and how the company handles damaged or incorrect items.

## Questions to ask before you place an order

A good supplier should be comfortable explaining the practical details. Ask how the product performs in direct sun, whether the glass helps reduce heat and glare, and what seals or drainage features are included. For sliding systems, ask to test the exact style of track and rollers if possible.

You should also ask who is responsible for final measurements. In most cases, the supplier should measure the openings after any demolition or structural work is complete. Ordering from rough drawings without a final site check can create costly sizing issues.

Clarify the warranty separately for the frame, glass, hardware, finish, and installation. Also ask what happens if a lock, hinge, roller, or seal needs replacing later. A local company with accessible after-sales service can be a major advantage over buying a cheaper product from a seller with limited installation support.

## Do not overlook glass, shading, and screens

In Cyprus, comfort is not only about the frame. Large south- and west-facing windows can bring in welcome light but also considerable solar heat. The best glass choice depends on orientation, room use, exterior shading, and whether you want to reduce glare without making interiors feel dark.

Discuss solar-control glazing, low-emissivity coatings, laminated safety glass, and acoustic options with your supplier. There is no universal best choice. A quiet bedroom near a road may need better sound control, while a shaded north-facing room may not need the same solar treatment as a west-facing living area.

Insect screens are another practical addition that newcomers often forget until the first warm evening. Retractable, fixed, sliding, and pleated systems are available, but not every style works with every window or door configuration. Include them in the original quote if you know you want them, rather than treating them as an afterthought.

## A practical route to buying with confidence

Begin by taking clear photos and rough measurements of each opening, noting whether it faces the road, sea, or strong afternoon sun. Visit local showrooms, request written quotes based on a site visit, and compare the full specification rather than the headline price. For larger jobs, bring your contractor or architect into the conversation before ordering.

The right door or window should suit how you actually live in Cyprus: open enough for breezes, secure when you travel, easy to clean, and built for the conditions outside your home. A supplier who asks careful questions before selling you anything is usually a very good sign.
